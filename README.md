# How do I approach designing software?

    What new design skills has your work on the project helped you to craft?
    What design process did you follow for your project work?
    How could tactics from your design approach be applied in future work?

Making a plan before diving into the code is helpful. For example, the process of identifying the objects from the 3D scene that will be replicated, identifying the shapes necessary to abstract the scene, and planning the various primitive 3D shapes for each object was necessary. Math was helpful to figure out things such as position in the space, or scale. Multi-part pieces like the stool can be planned out without jumping into the code, as it is all mathematically based. Then, using the plan, I can jump into the coding process, making edits or changes as the rendering reveals flaws or things missed during the planning process.

# How do I approach developing programs?

    What new development strategies did you use while working on your 3D scene?
    How did iteration factor into your development?
    How has your approach to developing code evolved throughout the milestones, which led you to the project’s completion?

A lot of the approach to the program was making educated guesses for various attributes, such as the XYZ position, scale, or rotations for objects. Then, it was an iterative process of running the application, seeing the result, then adjusting until I am happy with the result.

While the initial placement for objects were based on educated guesses for the XYZ positions, objects like the couch and the stool were easier to calculate due to their symmetry and shape. For example, after the first leg of the stool was placed, I could easily calculate the rest of the XYZ coordinate values for the legs and stool based on the scale I set each shape to, without needing to guess. To an extent, even creating the cat involved designing “one half” of his body, then using symmetry to set up the other half.
